Methodology

What is scored. Every 300 × 300 m cell of the four city cores (Limassol, Nicosia, Paphos, Larnaca) is scored as if a pizzeria stood at its centre. Cells with fewer than 200 residents and fewer than 3 shops or stops within reach are left blank (sea, hills, industrial land).

Signals (OpenStreetMap, formula cy-v1). Each signal is capped at a saturation threshold, scaled 0–1 and weighted:

Score = 100 × weighted sum, clipped to 0–100. Weights sum to 1.0 on the positive side, so a cell saturating every signal with no penalties scores 100.

Residents. Not census data: estimated from building footprints × floors (OSM building:levels or a default per building type) × 0.8 usable share ÷ m² per person by housing type (flats 40, houses 55). Low-rise suburbs therefore read lower than towers, which is what a delivery-and-walk-in pizzeria needs.

Why these weights. Cyprus cities are car-oriented, so transit is almost irrelevant (0.04 vs 0.16 in the Belgrade/Cluj version). Dense retail and food clusters on the seafront and in old centres are the real footfall, so shops, food places and the coastline bonus carry more; 20 competitors within 200 m is normal there, not a warning. Calibrated on our own units: Limassol-1 scores 81, Nicosia-1 78, Nicosia-2 42, Limassol-2 32.

Limits. The score measures how lively a place is, not Dodo demand: it does not see the Russian-speaking audience, delivery share or seasonality. Nicosia centre scores high while our unit there underperforms. A demand layer (aggregator or own delivery orders by address) is the next step.
